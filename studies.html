FOLIO 01 // LEAD RESEARCH

Neurodivergence & The Failure of Rigid Blockers

Hariadi, Chow & McGrenere · ACM CHI 2026 · DOI 10.1145/3772318.3790801

Interviews with 27 neurodivergent post-secondary students found tension between fixed, linear blocker designs and nonlinear work patterns such as hyperfocus.

Conventional blockers operate on rigid, all-or-nothing schedules that disregard executive constraints, time blindness, and the unique cognitive momentum of hyperfocus states (typical in ADHD and Autistic profiles). When users are abruptly locked out during flow, it shatters momentum and shames their capabilities, prompting them to abandon the tool completely.

CORE FINDINGS

I

Psychological Backlash

All-or-nothing blockers trigger executive shame and feelings of failure, reminding neurodivergent users of cognitive differences and fueling a cycle of negative self-evaluation.

II

The Stimming Failure

Blockers offer no middle ground for "digital stimming" (brief, controlled breaks to soothe overstimulated minds), driving users to uninstall or disable the apps out of frustration.

III

Hyperfocus Disruptions

Hard locks trigger abrupt task-switching. Because transitioning attention requires high executive function, a rigid schedule shatters momentum and triggers immediate abandonment.

02 / FLAWS

Why Traditional Strategies Fail

Four major behavioral and design flaws in conventional screen time utilities and gamified tracking tools.

Journal of Medical Internet Research

1. The Longitudinal “Rebound Effect” Study

+

Methodology: Multi-month parallel RCT tracking device usage and behaviors.

The Core Flaw: Forcing users into an app to monitor habits creates immediate device dependency.

The Backfire: Daily screen time actually increased ($P < .001$) as users rebounded from the tracking utility straight into doom-scrolling.

JMIR Parallel RCT Dataset
Frontiers in Psychiatry

2. The Fogg Behavior Model & The “Trigger Overload”

+

Methodology: Behavioral framework evaluation (Fogg & Hook Models) tracking sleep hygiene.

The Core Flaw: Passive warnings and "guilt dashboards" do not build user capability.

The Backfire: The notification itself acts as a digital trigger, drawing the user back to the screen and reinforcing the habit loop.

Frontiers in Psychiatry / NCC Report
University of Birmingham HCI Repository

3. Dual-Process Theory & The Conscious Resource Drain

+

Methodology: Behavioral studies analyzing conscious vs. nonconscious habit change.

The Core Flaw: Relying on conscious decision-making (System 2 thinking) to resist screen temptation.

The Backfire: Willpower is a finite resource. Under stress or fatigue, the brain defaults to automated habit execution, leading users to mindlessly bypass limits.

UoB HCI Repository
Bournemouth University DA Framework

4. The Peer-Group Compare & Deficit Model

+

Methodology: Persuasive system modeling of peer-group social tracking.

The Core Flaw: Using streaks and public comparisons to drive behavioral compliance.

The Backfire: Seeing peers maintain streaks while struggling internally damages self-esteem, creates shame, and leads users to delete the app.

BU Persuasive Systems Study
03 / CONSTRAINTS

Behavioral Physics & Lockouts

The psychological reactance and cognitive resource depletion mechanisms activated by coercive lockout software.

Behavioral Psychology

1. The Theory of Psychological Reactance

+

Mechanism: Restricting personal autonomy triggers Psychological Reactance.

The Core Flaw: The brain interprets unbreakable lockouts as a hostile threat to freedom.

The Backfire: Users experience an urgent impulse to reclaim autonomy, leading to uninstalls, clock tampering, or bypassing onto secondary devices.

Brehm, J. W. (1966) Reactance Model
Cognitive Energy & Self-Control

2. Self-Regulatory Fatigue (SRF)

+

Mechanism: Impulse suppression and manual app settings drain a user's trait self-control.

The Core Flaw: High administrative friction accelerates Self-Regulatory Fatigue (SRF).

External Stress
↓

↓
Executive Dysfunction
↓
Compulsive Relapse

The Backfire: Once self-control energy is depleted, executive dysfunction peaks, defaulting the brain to instant-gratification loops.

Longitudinal Student Cohort Studies
Georgetown University Research

3. The Moderation Paradox

+

Methodology: Evaluation of strict digital lockouts vs. moderate limits.

The Core Flaw: Demanding absolute behavioral compliance (e.g., strict detoxes).

What the evidence says: Reviews find that digital-detox interventions often reduce device use, while their effects on broader well-being remain mixed.

Design implication: Prefer flexible, measurable reductions over promising that one strict method works for everyone.

Digital Detox and Well-Being · Review (2024)
Nature Digital Medicine & JMIR

4. Fragmentation Fatigue

+

Methodology: Retention research consistently identifies long-term engagement as a central challenge for digital wellness products.

The Core Flaw: Fragmented app stacks (separate apps for limits, mindfulness, sleep) create high administrative fatigue.

The Backfire: Well-being apps use hooks (badges, streaks) that keep users on their screens, reinforcing device dependency.

Nature Digital Medicine / JMIR Datasets
