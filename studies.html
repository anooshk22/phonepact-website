Research Archive

Studies Backing Our Approach

A curated collection of peer-reviewed research informing our methodology.

01

Trauma & Autonomy

Research exploring the intersection of trauma exposure, autonomous self-regulation, and the capacity for conscious behavioral choice.

Biological Psychiatry

Effects of early life stress on cognitive and affective function: an integrated review of human literature

Pechtel, P., & Pizzagalli, D. A.

Extensive reviews indicate that early trauma disrupts the neural pathways required for emotion regulation, specifically shifting individuals away from autonomous control and toward reactive behavioral patterns.

Journal of Personality

Autonomy-supportive interventions and their impact on intrinsic motivation

Vansteenkiste, M., et al.

Clinical studies indicate that therapeutic and community environments that support autonomy, rather than applying restrictive control, are highly effective in helping trauma survivors recover their capacity for self-regulation.

American Psychologist

Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being

Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L.

Research applying Self-Determination Theory demonstrates that fulfilling basic psychological needs shifts individuals away from controlled or defensive behaviors toward autonomous self-regulation.

02

How Community Support Assists Addiction

Evidence for the sociological “looking-glass self” mechanism and how peer presence interrupts unconscious habitual behavior.

Substance Abuse and Rehabilitation

Benefits of peer support groups in the treatment of addiction

Tracy, K., & Wallace, S. P.

Research consistently shows that integrating peer support into conventional treatment improves patient engagement, motivation, and recovery outcomes by effectively countering isolation.

Addiction Research & Theory

Overcoming addiction as a process of social identity transition

Best, D., et al.

Group settings provide a "collective mission" where members become responsible not only to themselves but to their peers. This social tie serves as an informal system of monitoring that significantly lowers relapse risk.

Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews

Alcoholics Anonymous and other 12-step programs for alcohol use disorder

Kelly, J. F., et al.

Accountability systems create feedback loops that assist in habit formation. Regular engagement in recovery groups promotes future-oriented thinking by forcing individuals to pause and consult with their network before acting impulsively.

03

How Paid Services Cause an Increase in Retention

Research demonstrating the correlation between micro-financial commitment and sustained behavioral engagement.

Organizational Behavior

The psychology of sunk cost

Arkes, H. R., & Blumer, C.

The "sunk cost effect" describes the tendency to continue an endeavor once invested. In digital wellness, requiring payment—even a nominal fee—can lead to higher retention and better engagement than frictionless free models.

American Economic Journal

Incentives, commitments, and habit formation in exercise

Royer, H., Stehr, M., & Sydnor, J.

Studies demonstrate that combining incentives with self-funded commitment contracts (where users commit their own money to a goal) can lead to far more persistent behavioral changes and habit adherence.

National Bureau of Economic Research

What do Workplace Wellness Programs Do? Evidence from the Illinois Workplace Wellness Study

Jones, D., et al.

Evidence from large-scale wellness studies suggests that for behavioral health interventions, financial commitment can create persistent, resilient habits that last well beyond the initial incentive period.
