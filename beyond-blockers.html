Beyond blockers

For everyone who has out-smarted every lock they ever installed.

Every blocker fails for one structural reason: the rule-maker and the rule-breaker are the same person, alone, in private. A blocker is a door you locked while keeping the key in your own pocket, and the part of you that wants in knows exactly where the key is. That's why Apple ships an Ignore Limit button, why Opal subscriptions lapse, and why the pause in a friction app eventually becomes furniture.

The alternative isn't a stronger cage. It's changing what the moment is made of: elapsed time made visible while it passes, the feeling underneath the reach given a name, and one person who knows the intention you set. Private lapses are free, which is why they repeat. Witnessed ones have weight.

These guides cover the mechanism, the tools (compared honestly, including ours), and the practices that build self-regulation instead of renting it from software.
