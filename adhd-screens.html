ADHD and screens

Why the usual screen time advice was written for a different brain, and what to do instead.

If you have ADHD, or you're raising someone who does, the standard screen time advice has probably failed you, and the failure was structural, not personal. ADHD involves differences in dopamine signaling, which means an under-signaled reward system meets the most efficient stimulation-delivery device ever built. One large study of over 4,500 Korean adolescents found ADHD was the strongest correlate of smartphone addiction of any factor measured.

Blockers backfire harder here for three specific reasons: defeating them is a stimulating puzzle, hard blocking takes down the reminders ADHD life depends on, and every failure feeds a shame loop that drives more escape scrolling. What works instead is external structure: time made visible, stimulation replaced rather than removed, and accountability that doesn't rely on internal executive function, which is exactly the resource ADHD taxes.

These four guides cover the whole territory, from adult self-management to the meltdown at the end of a child's screen time.
