6 Best Opal Alternatives in 2026 (Picked by Why You're Leaving)

By Hank Yang. Harvard Divinity School, MTS '26 · Founder of PhonePact · Built internet businesses reaching millionsUpdated July 2026
The short answer

The short answer: choose your Opal alternative by why you're leaving. Price: Jomo (similar model, roughly a quarter of the cost) or ScreenZen (free). Rigidity: one sec's pause instead of a wall. Device coverage: Freedom. Focus sessions only: Forest. And if you left because you kept disabling Opal yourself, no stronger lock will fix that, which is the gap PhonePact (ours) is built for.

Opal is the category king of screen time apps and credit where it's due. The analytics are the best in the business and Deep Focus is a genuinely strong lock. But "Opal alternatives" is a popular search for a reason, and in my experience the reason is always one of these four:

  1. The price. Around $19.99 a month or $99.99 a year, which is two to four times the competition.
  2. The rigidity. Hard blocking starts to feel like being grounded by an app you're paying.
  3. iPhone only. No Android, no real laptop coverage.
  4. The quiet quit. You paid, you blocked, and three weeks later you'd disabled your own sessions and stopped opening the app entirely.

Disclosure up front: we make PhonePact, number six below. I'll be straight about where it fits and where it doesn't. The most useful way to pick isn't comparing feature grids. It's knowing which of those four complaints is yours. Prices approximate as of mid 2026.

1. Jomo. Opal, but cheaper

If you liked Opal's whole model, scheduled sessions, app categories, real enforcement, and the only problem was the invoice, Jomo is the closest thing to a discount Opal. Around $4.99 a month or $24.99 a year for a broadly similar feature set, with a clean interface and some fun touches like NFC tag unlocking.

Trade-offs: shallower analytics and a bit less polish in places. For a quarter of the price, most switchers stop noticing fast.

Switch to Jomo if the model worked and the price didn't.

2. ScreenZen. Stop paying until something works

ScreenZen is free. Actually free, not trial free. It swaps Opal's walls for configurable friction: a delay before an app opens, a cap on daily opens, tunable per app. Five seconds for email, ninety for TikTok.

It won't physically stop you and a determined thumb can outwait any timer. But as a zero-cost experiment in whether friction changes your behavior, nothing touches it. Plenty of people never need anything more.

Switch to ScreenZen if you want to cancel subscriptions while you figure out what actually helps you.

3. one sec. When the wall felt like punishment

Where Opal blocks, one sec breathes. Open a flagged app and you get a brief breathing exercise, then a question: still want in? It's the most research-validated approach in the category and reasonably priced at about $4.99 a month.

Two caveats. The pause is easy to breathe through on autopilot once the novelty fades. And the Apple Shortcuts setup is fiddlier than it should be. But if Opal's locked doors bred resentment in you, an open door with a doorbell might suit your temperament much better.

Switch to one sec if you respond to invitations, not restrictions. (Head to head: one sec vs Opal.)

4. Freedom. When your laptop is the loophole

Opal's blocking stops at your iPhone. If your scrolling just migrates to a browser tab, Freedom is the only mainstream tool that blocks apps and websites across phone, tablet, and computer in one synced session, at around $8.99 a month with cheaper annual and lifetime options.

It's more scheduler than habit-changer. You're planning lockdown windows, not rewiring impulses. But for cross-device leakage there's no real substitute.

Switch to Freedom if you close Instagram on your phone and open it on your Mac.

5. Forest. When you only ever needed focus blocks

Maybe you never needed an all-day warden. If your real problem is holding focus for defined stretches, studying, writing, deep work, Forest's grow-a-tree timer has been quietly excellent for a decade, for a one-time price of a few dollars. No subscription. The tree dies if you leave. You will care about the tree more than you expect to.

It does nothing between sessions, which is either a fatal flaw or exactly the point, depending on your problem.

Switch to Forest if your issue is focus sessions, not ambient all-day use.

Questions people ask

What's the cheapest Opal alternative?

ScreenZen is free. Among paid options, PhonePact at $0.99 a month after launch and Jomo at about $4.99 undercut Opal's roughly $19.99 substantially.

Is there an Opal alternative for Android?

Opal is iOS only and so are most alternatives here. Freedom covers Android, and one sec has an Android version too.

What's the best Opal alternative if blocking never sticks for me?

An accountability-based approach. If you consistently disable your own blockers, the missing ingredient is another person, not a stronger lock.

Opal vs one sec, which is better?

Different philosophies. Opal is a wall, hard sessions and deep analytics. one sec is a pause, breathing friction with no enforcement. Wall people find one sec toothless. Pause people find Opal oppressive. Know which you are before you pay either.
