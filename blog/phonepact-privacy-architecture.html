What PhonePact Measures, and What We Refuse To

By Hank Yang. Harvard Divinity School, MTS '26 · Founder of PhonePact · Built internet businesses reaching millionsUpdated July 2026
The short answer

On iPhone, PhonePact counts the Apple Screen Time scope you select. The Android development build counts whole-device foreground usage through Usage Access and has no app picker. Only coarse duration check-ins are shared with your circles; exact app, site, category, and per-app duration details stay on the phone. Each registered phone keeps its own pact and up to seven owner-only aggregate daily totals, so two phones are never merged. Circle room rows are swept after 24 hours. There are no ads or data sales.

Most posts on this blog mention our app once, at the end, and earn the rest with useful information. This one is different on purpose: it's the full disclosure document, written in plain language, because "trust us" is not an architecture. If you're evaluating any screen time app, including ours, these are the questions to ask, and here are our answers on the record.

What data does PhonePact actually collect?

Your measured activity and its duration. On iPhone, the app counts activity from the apps, categories, and websites you enroll through Apple’s picker. Selecting everything approximates a whole-device total; a smaller selection counts only that scope. On Android, the development build computes whole-device foreground usage through Usage Access and does not offer an app picker. Apple selection tokens and the app/site/category identities, raw events, and exact per-app durations used for measurement stay on the phone and are not uploaded. Private notices and Moment prompts are scheduled locally.

Your pact configuration and limited duration records. Your chosen daily point, circles, coarse shared check-in minutes, and room notes reach Firebase. Each registered phone has a random installation identifier, a short label such as “iPhone A1B2,” its own pact, and up to seven aggregate daily totals in a private device record so recent history survives signing out without being merged with another phone. The identifier is not a hardware serial, advertising ID, or fingerprint. Circle members can see the short label and pact point, but cannot read the daily totals. Room rows are removed by an hourly scheduled sweep once they are at least 24 hours old. A reported message is also copied into its safety report for review.

Account basics. We store what is needed to run the service: login, profile, and purchase status when billing is enabled. PhonePact is free for now during launch and beta. Pricing may change later; any paid plan and its terms will be shown before purchase. No ads, no data sales, no "partnerships" that are data sales in a trench coat.

the most trustworthy data policy is the data that was never collected.

What does my circle actually see?

One kind of event: "Alex's iPhone A1B2 reached 2h 30m today," at that phone's pact point and up to six later 30-minute milestones, with an optional short note if you chose to add one during your locally scheduled Moment. Circle members never see your app list, browsing, location, or owner-only seven-day totals. There are no leaderboards, streaks, or red badges of failure.

Pact raises are visible by design: once you have a real circle mate, you propose a longer goal and eligible people you select can answer it; the first yes or talk-first answer settles the request. Lowering remains yours alone, and only someone with no circle mates can self-raise. That's the one place we chose visibility over privacy, deliberately, because silent goal-abandonment is the failure mode the entire product exists to prevent, and an openly raised goal is the system working.

What will PhonePact never do?

No selling or sharing data with advertisers or brokers, ever. No location collection and no use of room content for advertising profiles. Room text is processed for safety filtering, and a reported message is retained with its report for review. Deleting your account removes the account, ordinary room notes and check-in rows attributed to you, and reports you filed. Reply excerpts and account-linked reactions or blocks are scrubbed. Reports about you may remain as anonymized safety records, with copied text attributed to you scrubbed. If any of this changes, it changes in the policy before it changes in the product.

We publish this because the screen time category has a trust problem it earned: apps positioned as digital wellness while harvesting granular behavioral data are surveillance with a lavender gradient. The bar you should hold any of them to, including us: what exactly is measured, who sees it, when is it deleted, and how does the company make money? Any app that can't answer all four in plain sentences has answered them.

Questions people ask

Does PhonePact see which apps I use?

No. App, site, and category identities and exact per-app duration stay on your phone. Firebase receives coarse shared check-in minutes and up to seven owner-only daily aggregate totals for each registered phone.

What do my circle members see in PhonePact?

Duration-only check-ins at your pact point and up to six later milestones, plus any short note you explicitly added. No app lists, location, private daily-total history, or rankings.

How does PhonePact make money?

PhonePact is free for now during launch and beta. Pricing may change later; any paid plan and its terms will be shown before purchase. There are no ads, data sales, or sponsored content.

Can I delete my PhonePact data?

Yes. Your account, ordinary room notes and check-in rows attributed to you, and reports you filed are deleted. Reports about you may remain as anonymized safety records, with copied text attributed to you scrubbed.
