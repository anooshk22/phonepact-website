How to Check Your Screen Time on iPhone (and How to Actually Read the Report)

By Hank Yang. Harvard Divinity School, MTS '26 · Founder of PhonePact · Built internet businesses reaching millionsUpdated July 2026
The short answer

The short answer: Settings, then Screen Time, then See All App and Website Activity. That screen shows your daily and weekly hours, your most-used apps, your pickups, and your notifications. Thirty seconds to find; the useful part is knowing which three numbers matter, your weekly trend, your pickups, and your first-app-after-pickup, and what to do once one of them bothers you.

This is the shortest post on this blog, because the task is genuinely simple and padding it would be an insult. Steps first, then the reading guide most articles skip.

Where do I find Screen Time on my iPhone?

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Screen Time.
  3. Tap See All App and Website Activity.

That's it. You'll land on a report with a Week and Day toggle. If Screen Time was never enabled, tap Turn On Screen Time first, then give it a few days to gather honest data. Two useful extras while you're there: Pickups (scroll down; how many times you physically checked the phone, and which app you opened first each time) and Notifications (which apps are interrupting you, and how often). You can also add the Screen Time widget to your home screen for an ambient daily readout, and if you use Family Sharing, each family member's report lives under their name in the same menu.

Which numbers in the report actually matter?

The report shows a dozen figures. Three carry nearly all the signal:

The weekly trend, not the daily total. Any single day lies: travel days, sick days, the Sunday you watched a season. The week-over-week direction is the honest number. Flat at a level that fits your life is fine; quietly climbing quarter after quarter is information. (For where your number sits against everyone else's, and why that's the wrong question, see average screen time.)

Pickups, and especially first-app-after-pickup. Hours tell you how much; pickups tell you how automatic. A hundred-plus daily checks with the same social app as your first destination is a habit loop with a name and an address. This single stat is the most underused diagnostic on the phone, and it's the backbone of the seven-day pickup experiment if you want to measure yourself properly.

Notifications received. Every notification is a paid interruption of your attention, and the report shows exactly who's buying. Any app sending you dozens a day that aren't humans trying to reach you is a settings fix away from silence.

the report can't judge you, it can only stop the guessing.

I checked it. Now what?

If the number surprised you, welcome to the majority: essentially everyone measures higher than they'd guessed, because phone time is engineered to be unfelt. The productive path from here is short. Watch the report for one full week without changing anything, so you have an honest baseline instead of a good-behavior week. Then act on what the three numbers showed: a climbing trend gets an intention set 15 to 20 percent under baseline; ugly pickups get the habit treatments; a loud notifications column gets pruned in ten minutes flat.

One warning from experience: the temptation at this point is to set App Limits right there in the same menu. They're free to try, and for most adults they fail in a specific, predictable way, there's a button called Ignore Limit and your thumb will learn it, which we've documented in why Apple Screen Time doesn't work for adults. The data half of Screen Time is excellent. The enforcement half was built for parents managing kids.

What actually moves the number, once you know it, is awareness plus company: your time made visible as it passes, and one person or a small circle who knows the daily point you chose and simply hears when you reach it. Questions people ask

How do I check screen time on iPhone?

Settings, then Screen Time, then See All App and Website Activity. The report shows daily and weekly totals, most-used apps, pickups, and notifications, with a Week/Day toggle at the top.

How do I see how many times I pick up my phone?

In the same report, scroll to Pickups. It shows total daily pickups, your average, and which app you opened first after each pickup, which is often the most revealing stat on the entire screen.

Why is my iPhone screen time so high?

Partly measurement honesty (everyone underestimates), and occasionally inflation from apps left open or audio apps counting background time. Check the per-app breakdown; if the hours are real, a one-week baseline plus a modest intention is the sane response, not panic.

Is iPhone Screen Time accurate?

Broadly yes for screen-on device use, with quirks: some background audio and CarPlay contexts can count oddly, and iPad or Mac usage merges in if Share Across Devices is on. Toggle that setting off in Screen Time if you want phone-only numbers.
