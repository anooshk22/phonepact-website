ABOUT THE AUTHOR

Hank Yang

I spent years building the machine. Now I work on the exit.

I built internet businesses reaching millions of people. My job, stated plainly, was capturing attention: making things people would open, and keep opening, and open again at midnight without quite deciding to. I was good at it. That's the part that stayed with me.

Then I went to Harvard Divinity School, where I finished an MTS in 2026 studying attention and consciousness. It's an unusual pairing on paper and an obvious one from the inside: the contemplative traditions have been asking what attention is, and what it costs to give it away, for a couple of thousand years longer than the ad industry has.

What I came away with is the premise underneath PhonePact. The problem was never your discipline. You are one tired person, alone, at 11pm, against systems designed by thousands of specialists to defeat exactly your intentions. That fight was rigged before you entered it. The only thing that reliably changes the odds is not fighting it alone.

So PhonePact does not block anything, shame anyone, or count streaks. You set your own daily point. When you reach it, a few people you trust simply know. They see the time you chose to share, and nothing else, ever. Here is exactly what we measure and what we refuse to collect.

I write Notes on Attention, which is forty-odd field guides on this whole territory: ADHD and screens, the family phone war, and what to do when every blocker has failed you. Everything I cite is real and hedged honestly; the studies are listed here.

If something on this site helped, or got something wrong, I'd like to know. Tell me.
