What is PhonePact?

The short version, the mechanics, and the things it deliberately will not do.

PhonePact is a mobile app that helps you reduce your own screen time. You choose a daily amount of phone time — your pact point — and trusted circles can receive duration-only shared check-ins at that point and up to six later milestones. It never blocks an app, never locks your phone, and never tells anyone what you were doing.

It is made by PhonePact LLC, an independent company founded in 2026 by Hank Yang. It is not a parental-control app, and there is no version of it that lets one person monitor another.

Everything below is the same information in more detail. If you'd rather read the argument than the spec, that lives in our ethos.

How it works

THREE MOVING PARTS

1. You set a pact point for each phone. A number of hours and minutes in what that phone counts — the point at which you'd like to notice you've been on it. On iPhone, you choose an Apple Screen Time scope. On Android, you choose the whole phone or individual launchable apps in PhonePact’s private on-device picker. It is a threshold, not a limit: nothing happens to your phone when you cross it. Lowering it is yours alone. Raising it needs a stated duration and, once you have any real circle mate, approval from eligible people you select. The first yes settles the request. The first Talk first response claims a one-to-one pact discussion and pauses everyone else until that responder decides or the request closes. Hiding every mate does not create a bypass. If you genuinely have no circle mates, you can raise it alone.

2. Time counts upward, and a private notice reaches you first. PhonePact counts that phone’s configured usage and only ever states time already spent, never time remaining. On iPhone, selecting everything in Apple’s picker approximates the whole-device total; a smaller selection counts only that scope. On Android, Usage Access counts the whole phone or the apps you selected. Your own Home screen can show a private “Where it went” shortlist of up to five selected apps used for at least a minute today. Ten usage-minutes before your pact point, your phone schedules a private notice that nobody else sees. You can choose a 20-, 30-, 45-, or 60-minute notice rhythm, or turn notices off.

3. Your circle gets plain duration-only sentences attributed to the phone that measured them. At that phone's pact point, PhonePact can share something like "Hank's iPhone A1B2 reached 2 hours of screen time today." The same kind of check-in can repeat every additional thirty minutes, up to six more times — as many as seven shared check-ins in a day. There is no final state, no streak and no score. Each circle has a plain-text room for encouragement. Ordinary rows expire after 24 hours; any current member can visibly keep a member-written message for everyone for up to 30 days. Current members who share a circle can also talk one-to-one: ordinary private messages fade after 24 hours, while a visibly kept message lasts 30 days.

What PhonePact will not do

BY DESIGN

It will not block, lock, or restrict any app. There is no limit to hit, no wall to argue with, and no override code — because there is nothing to override. This is deliberate: a lock you control is a door you locked while keeping the key in your own pocket.

It will not show your circle what you used the phone for. The duration is the only usage information in a shared check-in. It is attributed to you and may include a short phone label when needed to distinguish phones, plus a short note you deliberately add, but never apps, sites, messages, or location. Your private “Where it went” summary is visible only on that phone. On iPhone, Apple selection details stay on the phone; on Android, package-level Usage Access details stay on the phone and are not sent.

It will not let one person monitor another. Everyone in a circle is a peer running the app on their own device, and everyone sees the same single fact about everyone else. There is no dashboard, no admin, no parent side.

It will not rank, score, or streak you. No leaderboards, no comparisons, no penalty for a bad day. The research on social comparison is the reason.

It will not sell your data or show you ads. Ever. That's what the subscription is for.
