Legal

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 September 2026

The short version. PhonePact helps you use your phone less with support from circles you choose. We deliberately keep server collection narrow and never sell personal information. A shared check-in contains a coarse duration (for example, “reached 2h today”), the member and phone that recorded it, its time, and any short note the member deliberately adds. Your circles never receive your app list, exact per-app activity, selected websites, browsing history, private Insights reports, or owner-only daily history. You can ask us to access or delete data we control.

This Privacy Policy explains how PhonePact LLC (“PhonePact,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) handles information when you use the PhonePact mobile application, the getphonepact.com website, and related services (together, the “Service”). Where applicable privacy law uses those terms, PhonePact LLC is the controller or business responsible for the processing described here.

What “private” means here. Some information stays only on your phone; other information is stored in an owner-only part of our Firebase backend or is visible only to the people you choose. Server-hosted information must still be processed by PhonePact and its service providers to operate the Service. “Private” does not mean end-to-end encrypted or inaccessible to authorized operators unless we expressly say it stays on-device.

1. Information we handle

Account, profile, and authentication

  • Name, email address, and authentication data — Firebase Authentication handles email/password sign-in and provider sign-in. If you use or link Google or Apple, we receive the provider identifier and any name or email the provider makes available. We store verification status, linked sign-in methods, and a limited verification-grace timestamp used to secure sharing. PhonePact does not store your plaintext password in its own databases. Linking another method requires your action and does not automatically merge two separate PhonePact accounts merely because their emails match.
  • Sign-in provider security data — Google Sign-In can process technical identifiers, device/app information, and IP-derived approximate location for sign-in, security, and abuse prevention. This is separate from phone-location permission; PhonePact does not request precise location to measure screen usage. Provider handling is also subject to that provider’s privacy policy.
  • Profile and circle appearance — your optional avatar, profile details, and a circle photo you choose to contribute are shown to the relevant circle members.
  • Hand-drawn pact signature — stroke-path data used only to render your Phone Pact. It is not analyzed for identity, used as a legal signature, or used for biometric recognition or authentication.
  • Onboarding answers, goals, and settings — the intentions, pact point, notice choices, and other settings you enter to configure the Service.
  • Account and device identifiers — a Firebase account ID and a random per-installation ID. The installation ID is not a hardware serial number, advertising ID, or device fingerprint. A short label such as “iPhone A1B2” helps distinguish phones on the same account.

Circles, pact activity, and content

  • Circle and membership data — circle names, appearance, invitations, membership, blocks, and the people involved.
  • Pact and change-request data — pact points, which eligible circle members you ask, reasons or notes you provide, responses, timestamps, and request status. App-selection identities are not included in a what-counts request.
  • Room messages and interactions — short notes, replies, reactions, and visible Keep attribution. Ordinary rows expire after 24 hours. A current circle member may visibly keep a member-written message for everyone until no later than 30 days after it was sent.
  • Private conversation messages — one-to-one text and pact context shared with a current member of a circle you share. Ordinary messages expire after 24 hours; either participant may visibly keep a message for up to 30 days.
  • Reports, blocks, feedback, and support — report reason, reported content and context, reporter/subject identifiers, moderation status, blocks, and messages or screenshots you deliberately send to PhonePact for feedback or support.
  • Deterministic safety screening — limited server rules inspect text entered as profile or display names, circle names, phone labels, Room/private messages, optional check-in notes, and reasons for pact or What Counts requests for blocked safety terms or patterns. This is rule-based processing, not AI analysis or advertising profiling, and it can make mistakes. Depending on the field, blocked required text is rejected or sanitized; blocked optional notes or reasons can be omitted while the underlying action continues.

Screen-usage and check-in information

With your permission, PhonePact uses the screen-usage system on your phone. On iPhone, Apple’s Screen Time, Family Controls, and Device Activity features count the scope you select, which may include the whole device or selected apps, categories, and websites. On Android, Usage Access lets you choose the whole phone or individual launchable apps in an on-device picker. Home can show a private “Where it went” summary of up to ten selected apps used for at least one minute that day.

App/package identities, selection tokens, exact per-app activity, website identities, and raw usage events are not sent to PhonePact’s servers or to your circles. PhonePact sends owner-only aggregate totals and coarse events needed to preserve recent history and operate shared check-ins. A qualifying shared check-in contains the account and circle identifiers, the measuring phone’s ID and short label, the event time, a duration threshold, and an optional note. Subject to synchronization and stale/replay suppression, a phone can create one at its pact point and at later 30-minute crossings through 1,410 minutes (23h 30m) of measured use. At the smallest 30-minute pact point, that is as many as 47 potential shared check-ins in a day. The private pre-check-in Moment/Talk First prompt is limited to the pact point and six later crossings; that prompt limit does not limit the shared check-in ladder. Each registered phone has its own pact and up to seven recent recorded dates of aggregate totals in an owner-only Firebase device record. Some older accounts may also retain a legacy owner-only copy of recent totals until account deletion. Circle members cannot read either owner-only history.

Pending iPhone relay outbox. A protected App Group file can temporarily hold an unsent relay item containing account and phone identifiers, event kind and identifier, timestamps, minutes, local day and time-zone context, Activity binding, and, where applicable, circle context or a deliberately added note. The backend rejects relay events more than 14 days old. Delivered, invalid, stale, or over-age items are removed on later outbox processing, so an inactive local file can physically remain until that cleanup path or account teardown runs. It does not contain app, package, category, or website identities.

Optional live status

Android can display aggregate time and pact progress locally in a widget or supported live-status surface. On supported iPhones, an optional Live Activity can continue updating while the App is not open. To route it, PhonePact sends its backend account/device IDs, ActivityKit and push routing tokens, an activity ID and state, local day, whole-minute aggregate elapsed-time floors, observation/start/dismissal times, time-zone offset, pact point, and delivery/retry metadata. This routing state is owner-only and is sent through Firebase Cloud Messaging and Apple Push Notification service. It contains no app, category, website, or browsing identity and is never shown to a circle. You can disable or dismiss the surface; an already accepted in-flight push cannot be recalled.

Supporter Insights

Optional monthly and one-time PhonePact Supporter purchases unlock Deeper Insights in You → Insights in builds where the feature is available. Views can include daily, weekly, monthly, time-of-day, app-level, trend, comparison, research-context, and pact-journal cards. Measurements vary by phone, operating-system version, permission, selection, and available history. Missing days are not treated as zero, and the metrics do not establish intent, focus, sleep, diagnosis, or why usage changed.

iPhone. Apple supplies the selected Screen Time data directly to a protected report extension. That report may transiently process daily/hourly aggregate time, app identities and durations, pickups, notification counts, first pickup, and longest activity when Apple supplies them. Selected category or website activity may contribute to aggregate totals, but the current PhonePact report does not list, export, or store visited domain names. Detailed report values are rendered inside Apple’s protected extension and are not copied into the main App, PhonePact servers, or RevenueCat. An opaque selection token stays on-device; it may survive sign-out so the same account can restore its selection and is cleared by account deletion.

Android. If you affirmatively choose to save private history, PhonePact reads local Android usage events, including which app/package was used and when. It stores private no-backup summaries containing daily/hourly totals, app labels and categories, pseudonymous package keys, per-app duration, visit/session and quick-return estimates, unlocks, app switches, first/last activity, and screen-off break estimates. Raw Android usage history remains controlled by Android. PhonePact’s summary files remain on that phone, are excluded from Android backup/device transfer, and are not uploaded.

A separate Android notification option, where offered or previously enabled, requires both an in-app choice and Android Notification Access. PhonePact counts eligible arrivals by app, using a deduplication key and post time, without reading or storing notification text, title, sender, actions, icons, conversation content, or extras. Ongoing notifications and group summaries are excluded. Permission gaps and platform behavior can make counts incomplete.

A small on-device pact journal is maintained for signed-in accounts so recent pact settings and crossings recorded on that phone are ready if Insights is unlocked. It stores event kind, time, and minutes, not message text or a complete approval/delivery history. Insights calculations are deterministic and on-device: PhonePact does not use an AI service to analyze them, analyze messages, rank members, or send detailed Insights, app identities, or reports to circles, RevenueCat, or advertising/behavioral analytics services.

Technical, diagnostic, push, and update information

  • Push and routing tokens — Firebase Cloud Messaging and, where used, Apple push and ActivityKit addresses let us send remote circle, pact-request, private-conversation, shared-check-in, and Live Activity updates. Private-conversation pushes identify the sender but omit message text. Private notices and Moment prompts are scheduled locally.
  • Crash and diagnostic reports — release builds use Firebase Crashlytics. Reports are linked to the Firebase account ID and may include fatal/nonfatal errors, native or JavaScript stack traces, error messages, limited component-stack or developer log context, timestamps, app/build identifiers, Crashlytics/Firebase installation and session IDs, device model/specifications, operating system, and whether the App was in the foreground. We do not intentionally add message bodies or app-selection details to crash reports, but unexpected error context can contain information involved in the failing operation.
  • Service and security logs — IP address, user agent, request time, app version, device/OS type, response/error information, and security or integrity signals can be processed by PhonePact, Firebase, Apple, Google, or Expo when you authenticate, call the backend, receive a push, or check for an app update. PhonePact’s backend operational lines can also contain account and random device/installation IDs; circle, request, message, push-token/claim, relay, or delivery-job IDs; and failure or retry details needed to diagnose integrity and delivery problems. We do not intentionally put message bodies, app/site selections, or Insights content in those operational lines. Our default Cloud Logging bucket retains entries for 30 days; Google Cloud’s required audit-log bucket retains its audit entries for 400 days. These log copies are not individually found and scrubbed by in-app account deletion, even after the underlying PhonePact records are removed.
  • Safety-alert logs — a filed report creates a Cloud Logging alert so a human can act. Since 27 September 2026, the report-alert line sends only a one-way lookup code, report type, and filing revision to that log; the report content stays in the access-controlled report record. Earlier backend revisions copied report reasons, content, names, and raw account/circle identifiers into the alert log. Any legacy entries remain subject to the default bucket’s 30-day retention and are not individually scrubbed by the in-app account-deletion flow. Copies already delivered to an alert recipient follow that recipient’s notification-service retention; deleting the account does not recall a delivered alert.
  • Over-the-air update data — Expo/EAS Update receives ordinary network and app/runtime/platform metadata needed to determine and deliver a compatible over-the-air update.

Supporter purchases and RevenueCat

The core experience is currently available without a Supporter purchase. Apple or Google Play sells optional Supporter purchases and handles payment credentials; PhonePact does not receive or store your card or bank details. PhonePact uses RevenueCat as its purchase processor. When RevenueCat is enabled in a build, the SDK identifies each signed-in user with the raw Firebase account ID so it can check access, even if that user has not purchased. RevenueCat may process that account ID; app, SDK, device/OS, locale/currency, last-seen, network/approximate-country, and permitted device-identifier information; and, when applicable, product/offer, Apple receipt or Google purchase token, transaction, purchase date, renewal/expiration, subscription, and entitlement information.

PhonePact uses this information to show localized offerings, validate purchases, prevent purchase fraud, restore and synchronize access, provide billing support, and produce purchase/subscription analytics. Our own Firebase profile mirrors only whether purchases are available and whether Supporter access is active; it does not receive the store receipt. PhonePact app code does not set RevenueCat customer attributes for your name, email, phone number, screen usage, pact, messages, selection, or Insights. RevenueCat does not receive those details from PhonePact. We do not use purchase data for cross-app advertising or tracking.

4. Retention and deletion

  • Owner-only server usage. Active code keeps the newest seven recorded dates of aggregate totals per phone, not a seven-calendar-day time-to-live. Intermediate iPhone relay/Live Activity state can include additional current-day whole-minute floors needed for synchronization and delivery. Relay receipts expire after about 15 days; relay credentials expire after no more than 180 days; completed/invalid delivery jobs and superseded token claims are removed. The current device routing state otherwise remains with the device record until replaced, the device is revoked, or the account is deleted. A legacy owner-only total copy may remain until deletion. None of this contains app or website identities.
  • Android on-device Insights. After fresh consent in expanded builds, usage summaries can accumulate up to 365 recorded days. Day entries outside that window are pruned when the archive refreshes; an abandoned/inactive file can physically remain until the cleanup path runs. Notification-count buckets cover up to 28 recorded days and prune when another eligible notification is saved. Turning off and clearing private history deletes usage summaries and notification counts but not the pact journal. Ordinary sign-out pauses measurement and clears the extended Insights summaries, notification counts, and pact journal, but preserves an account-scoped native resume checkpoint containing the selected package list, pact/progress settings, and up to 14 recorded dates of aggregate history for restoration to that same account on that installation. Revoking an OS permission stops new access but does not by itself erase prior summaries. Account deletion, clearing app data, or uninstalling removes the local checkpoint.
  • Supporter expiry. Expiration closes paid reports but does not delete history or revoke OS permissions. On Android, previously enabled private-history refresh and notification counting can continue after paid access expires. The always-available You → Profile → Private Insights data control stops that collection and deletes PhonePact’s saved usage summaries and notification counts without unlocking paid reports. You can also stop new access by revoking the relevant permission, signing out, clearing app data, deleting the account, or uninstalling.
  • Local aggregate resume data. While measurement is active, native iPhone and Android stores can keep up to 14 recorded dates of aggregate duration as rollover/offline slack for the seven-date ledger. Both apps also keep an account-and-installation-scoped cache of up to seven aggregate dates across ordinary sign-out so the same account can resume without losing recent totals. These caches contain duration and date, not app identities, and clear for that account during account deletion.
  • iPhone on-device data. Detailed Screen Time history is supplied and retained by Apple; it is not copied to a PhonePact detailed-history store. Revoking access stops PhonePact from reading new reports but does not delete Apple’s OS-held history. Ordinary sign-out clears the active PhonePact App Group aggregate history, while the seven-date account cache and opaque selection token may survive for same-account restoration; both clear on account deletion.
  • Pact journal. The local journal keeps no more than 28 recent days and 300 entries, prunes on read/write, and clears at sign-out or account teardown.
  • Shared check-in source events. Account-linked coarse events, including their timestamp, phone/circle context, minutes, and optional note, are retained until account deletion separately from the short-lived Room row. They do not contain app identities or exact per-app usage and are not a complete daily history.
  • Messages. Ordinary Room and private messages expire after 24 hours. A visibly kept member-written Room message or private message expires no later than 30 days after sending. PhonePact clients stop displaying a row at its expiry time; scheduled server deletion runs hourly and normally removes it on the next successful sweep, but a backlog or failed job can delay physical removal. Releasing the last Room Keep restores ordinary expiry without reviving an expired message. Deleting an account deletes its room notes/check-in rows and its private conversations, and scrubs account-linked reply excerpts, reactions, Keep attribution, block references, and names.
  • Safety reports. A reported-message copy remains available while a report is open. It becomes eligible for deletion 90 days after review closes; a daily server sweep normally removes it on the next successful run, but a failed job can delay physical removal. Reports filed by a deleted account are deleted. Reports about a deleted account may remain as safety records with the account identifier replaced by an opaque value and copied name/text attributable to that user scrubbed. Those deletion actions apply to the Firestore report record. Legacy Cloud Logging alert copies described above follow the logging bucket’s separately configured retention and are not individually scrubbed by account deletion.
  • Account/profile data. We keep it while the account is active. In-app deletion starts deletion of PhonePact-hosted account, profile, device, membership, pact, private-conversation, feedback, entitlement-flag, and usage records, plus Room rows attributed to or about the deleted account. It also clears a current shared-circle photo while that photo is still attributed to the deleted account. A shared circle and content belonging to its remaining members continue; if nobody remains, the circle and invite-code mapping are queued for removal. A content-free deletion guard keyed to the former account ID remains for seven days to stop delayed devices from recreating deleted data. Google states that Firebase Authentication data can take up to 180 days to leave its live and backup systems after deletion is initiated.
  • Crash reports. Google states that Crashlytics keeps crash traces and associated installation identifiers for 90 days before beginning removal from live and backup systems.
  • Purchase records. PhonePact’s two Firebase entitlement flags delete with the account. After PhonePact account cleanup, our backend requests deletion of the matching RevenueCat customer and retries requests that fail. RevenueCat processes deletion asynchronously, so deletion is not immediate. If you need confirmation or the automatic request cannot be matched, contact us for a manual provider request. Deleting its customer record does not delete an app store’s transaction record or cancel a subscription. RevenueCat and the stores may retain records under their policies and as needed for entitlement, refund, fraud, accounting, tax, or law. If the PhonePact account is already gone and no automatic deletion can be confirmed, an exact production Google transaction ID or eligible Apple order ID may be needed to locate the record; PhonePact sends RevenueCat no email customer attribute, so email alone may not identify it. Any manual request depends on unambiguous record matching, provider availability, required retention, and law.
  • Website submissions. We keep testing/waitlist, SMS-consent, feedback, and support records only while needed for the requested communication, support, safety, or legal purpose, unless you ask us to delete them sooner and no exception applies.

Google Apps Script records the primary submission in Google Sheets. If JavaScript is unavailable or the primary result cannot be confirmed and you choose the displayed backup form, Formspree processes it instead.

The current site does not load an advertising or analytics tracker and does not set advertising cookies. GitHub Pages hosts the site, and Google Fonts plus Apple/Google store-badge or linked resources may receive ordinary HTTP data such as IP address, user agent, referrer subject to browser policy, and request time when your browser requests them. Invite links are access capabilities. New links put the six-character invite after #. Browsers do not include that fragment in the HTTP request to GitHub Pages, and the page is designed to remove it from the visible address after reading it. Older ?c= links remain supported, but their full request URL, including the code, reaches GitHub Pages before page code can remove it and can remain in hosting/security logs under GitHub’s configured practices. The page’s no-referrer rule prevents the invite URL from being sent onward as a referrer to fonts, store badges, or followed links; it cannot hide a legacy query from the site host or erase a copy already made.

Either link form can also remain in the sender’s or recipient’s messages, link previews, clipboard, screenshots, and browser history or synchronization. PhonePact cannot set retention for or delete those outside copies, and in-app account deletion does not remove them. An invite code remains usable while its circle exists; it can remain usable after one member deletes their account if other members keep the circle, and the current App has no code-rotation or revocation control. For least exposure, send only the six-character code and enter it directly in the App, then delete message, clipboard, and browser-history/sync copies you control. Clearing those copies does not revoke the active code. GitHub’s handling is described in its Privacy Statement.
